# Image-Gen Prompts

Concept image only; not fabrication authority. Do not include cut-ready dimensions, hole locations, or toolpaths.

## Hero Prompt

A single slip-cast ceramic udu drum — a closed, full-bellied Nigerian clay vessel drum — photographed as a finished physical object on a clean neutral light-grey studio sweep. The body is a rounded, slightly egg-shaped stoneware pot that narrows to a short open neck and a circular mouth opening at the top; a single round side hole is cut into the swell of the belly, its rim smoothed and slightly flared for repeated hand slaps. Fired Cone-6 stoneware with a satin-matte glazed exterior in a warm earthen tone (clay-brown to dark slate), the rim of the mouth and the edge of the side hole left rounded and comfortable, the unglazed interior implied by the dark void inside the mouth. Subtle, gentle foot pads or a flattened base let it sit stably on the surface. Realistic tabletop scale — a vessel roughly the size of a large floor pot. Soft directional studio light from upper left, gentle falloff and a soft shadow, photorealistic, 3/4 product-photography angle, ~50mm lens, high detail, no text labels.

## In-Context Prompt

A ceramic udu drum staged in a percussionist's practice space or a ceramics studio, sitting on a low wooden stool or woven mat, warm natural window light raking across the glazed belly. A pair of relaxed, anatomically plausible hands hover near the mouth and the side hole as if mid-phrase — one palm poised to slap the side port, one fanning over the mouth to bend the pitch. Background softly out of focus: shelves with bisque pots, a kiln door, or stage curtains, telling the story of a fired ceramic instrument that is played by sealing and opening its two ports. Shallow depth of field, golden ambient light, concept visualization only, photorealistic, no text labels.

## Detail Prompt

Macro close-up of the udu's signature side hole — the round port cut into the swell of the stoneware belly — showing the smoothed, gently flared lip meant for repeated hand slaps, the satin glaze surface meeting the rounded port edge, and the dark unglazed interior receding into shadow beyond. Raking low-angle light reveals the subtle surface texture of fired stoneware, faint throwing-style undulation, and the crisp clean transition from glazed exterior to bare clay at the hole edge. Extremely high detail, shallow depth of field, no measurements, no text.

## Negative / Avoid

No text, logos, watermarks, or fake maker marks; no dimension callouts or measurement lines; no drumheads, skins, tension rods, or membrane-drum hardware; no impossible mechanisms; if hands are shown they must be anatomically plausible.
